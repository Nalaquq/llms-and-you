Key Terms, Weeks 1–7

Every study-guide entry so far, in a line or two. Use it to find what you cannot yet define; then read the full entry, which also says what you should be able to do with the idea.

Week 1 design vocabulary

Affordance
what a designed thing makes possible for the person using it.
Constraint
what it prevents, on purpose. A prompt is where you add the constraints a chat box leaves out.
Signifier
the visible clue that an affordance is there at all.

Week 2 how text becomes numbers

The machinery words

Corpus
the body of text a method learns from during training. Afterwards only its effects remain.
Vocabulary
the fixed list of items a method can represent. Anything else is out of vocabulary.
Vector
an ordered list of numbers of fixed length: a point in a space, and an arrow with a direction.
Dimensionality
how many numbers are in each vector.
Neural network, layer
a stack of layers, each turning one list of numbers into another. “Deep” means many layers.
Parameters (weights)
the numbers inside the layers that training adjusts. What “open weights” releases.
Training / inference
training sets the parameters once from a corpus; inference is every use of the finished model.
Softmax
turns any list of scores into probabilities between 0 and 1 that add up to 1.

Text into numbers

Token, tokenization
cutting text into subword pieces from the model's vocabulary. A token is not a word.
One-hot encoding
a vector as long as the vocabulary with a single 1 at the word's position.
Sparse / dense vector
sparse: long and mostly zeros (one-hot, counts, TF-IDF). Dense: short, every entry used (embeddings).
Embedding
a token turned into a dense vector whose numbers carry something about meaning.

The geometry

Embedding space
the shared space one model's embeddings live in, where similar meaning becomes “close together”.
Dot product
multiply two vectors entry by entry and add. Large when they point the same way; also grows with length.
Cosine similarity
the dot product divided by both lengths, leaving only the angle: 1 same direction, 0 unrelated, −1 opposite.
Nearest neighbors
the k vectors closest to a given one, usually by cosine similarity.
Dimensionality reduction
squashing vectors to two or three dimensions so they can be drawn (PCA, t-SNE, UMAP).
Vector arithmetic
differences between vectors encode relationships (king − man + woman ≈ queen), with real limits.

Counting methods

Bag of words
a document as the words it contains and how often, with order discarded.
Count vectorization
one column per term, one row per document, counts in the cells: a document-term matrix.
Stop words
very common words (“the”, “of”) removed before counting.
Stemming
chopping endings off by rule. Fast; the result need not be a real word.
Lemmatization
reducing a word to its dictionary form using a vocabulary. Always a real word.
N-grams
runs of adjacent words counted as units: bigrams (two), trigrams (three).
Term frequency (TF)
how often a term appears in one document, divided by the document's length.
Inverse document frequency (IDF)
how rare a term is across the corpus. Zero for a term in every document.
TF-IDF
TF × IDF: high when a term is frequent here and rare elsewhere.

Learned embeddings

Distributional hypothesis
words that appear in similar contexts have similar meanings. A bet, not a theorem.
Sliding window
a fixed-width window moved along the text; each stop is one training example.
CBOW
predict the middle word from its neighbors. Context in, one word out.
Skip-gram
predict the neighbors from the middle word. One word in, context out.
Negative sampling
train against a few random wrong words instead of a softmax over the whole vocabulary.
word2vec
the 2013 model that learns one dense vector per word with CBOW or skip-gram.
Pretext task
a training task nobody wants the answers to, set so the network must learn a useful representation.

Context and sequences

Polysemy
one word with several meanings (“bank”). Ordinary, not exotic.
Encoder / decoder
an encoder reads a sequence into a representation; a decoder writes a sequence from one.
Pretraining / fine-tuning
train once on a huge general corpus, then continue on a small task-specific one.
Masked language modeling
BERT's task: hide some words and recover them using the words on both sides.
BERT
an encoder-only transformer (2018) that reads text and produces representations.
Static / contextual embedding
static: one fixed vector per word (word2vec). Contextual: computed per occurrence from the sentence.
Recurrent neural network (RNN)
reads one item at a time, updating a running summary.
Hidden state
that running summary: a fixed-size vector, however much has been read.
Sequence-to-sequence
an encoder and a decoder joined only by the encoder's final hidden state, the context vector.
Fixed-length bottleneck
the whole input squeezed into that one vector. The problem attention solved.

The output end

Logits
the raw scores a model produces, one per token in the vocabulary, before softmax.
Temperature
a number the logits are divided by. Higher flattens the choice; zero always takes the top token.

Week 3 attention and the transformer

Architecture / model / technique
a design (the transformer); one trained instance of it (BERT); a method used on or around a model (a prompt).
Attention (self-attention)
every word looks at every other word in one step and is adjusted by what it finds.
Query
the question a word asks of the others.
Key
the label a word wears, matched against other words' queries.
Value
what a word hands over to any word that attends to it.
Attention score
the dot product of one word's query with another's key.
Attention weights
a row of scores after softmax: shares that add up to 1. Attention is a budget.
Multi-head attention
several heads side by side in a layer, each with its own learned questions.
Positional encoding
a vector marking a token's position, added to its embedding so word order is not lost.
Causal mask
scores for later words set to minus infinity before softmax, so a decoder cannot read ahead. Weights are exactly zero.
Cross-attention
queries from the decoder, keys and values from the encoder: how the writing half looks at what was read.
Transformer block
attention, then a feed-forward network, with “add and normalize” after each. Stacked many times.
Add and normalize
the block's input is added back to its output, so nothing is fully overwritten.
What the transformer traded
everything at once (parallel training) for cost that grows with the square of the length. Generation is still one token at a time.
Attention is not explanation
a weight shows how one head blended values, not why the model produced its output.

Week 6 chain of thought and its critics

Zero-shot / few-shot
a prompt with no worked examples, or with one or more (exemplars) before the question.
Chain-of-thought prompting
asking for intermediate steps before the final answer.
Benchmark
a fixed set of questions with known answers that everyone scores on.
Emergent ability
absent in small models, appearing above some size.
Ablation study
remove or replace one part at a time and compare with the full version (the control).
Training / test distribution
the pattern the training examples follow, and the pattern of the held-back examples used to check.
Out-of-distribution (OOD)
questions outside the pattern of the training data. Doing well on them is OOD generalization.
Data leakage
test questions or answers turning up in training data, so memory cannot be told from method.
Exact match
right only if identical to the expected answer.
Edit distance
the fewest single-character changes that turn one string into another. Lower is better.
BLEU
a score for how many short runs two texts share. 1 is identical.
Faithfulness
whether the steps a model writes are how it actually reached its answer.
Loss / risk
the penalty for one answer; the average penalty. Empirical risk is over the questions seen, expected risk over all a distribution could produce.
Total variation distance
how different two distributions are: 0 identical, 1 no overlap.
Generalization bound
a guarantee that test risk is at most training risk plus stated costs. A ceiling, not a prediction.

Week 7 truthfulness and evaluation

Hallucination
a fluent, confident statement that is false or cites something that does not exist.
Imitative falsehood
a false answer given because the model learned its training text well: the popular myth is the likely continuation.
Inverse scaling
a result that gets worse as models get larger, as truthfulness did on TruthfulQA in 2021.
Truthful
avoids asserting anything false. “I have no comment” is truthful.
Informative
actually addresses what was asked. The number that matters is answers that are both.
Ground truth
the answer an evaluation treats as correct: checked, sourced, and written by a person.
Evaluation set
a fixed list of inputs with ground truth that every version of a prompt is run against.
Marking rule
the rule that turns an answer and the ground truth into right or wrong. Part of your definition of correct.
Inter-annotator agreement
how far two people marking the same answers give the same marks.
Cohen's kappa
agreement corrected for chance: 1 perfect, 0 no better than chance.
Small-sample uncertainty
with twenty items, one item is five points and any score has a wide plausible range.
