# Individual meetings

Five sessions this term are **individual meetings** rather than a group class.
Each student gets a scheduled 15-minute slot, and that week's work is
self-paced.

{{ conference_table() }}

They appear on the [schedule](../schedule.md) like any other session, marked
:material-account-clock-outline:.

---

## Booking your slot

A sign-up sheet goes out the **Thursday before**, with times across the whole
week — including some outside the normal class hour. Pick one.

If nothing on the sheet works, email me. There is almost always another time.

Attending counts toward your [participation
grade](../assignments.md#participation) exactly as attending class does.

---

## What to bring

Fifteen minutes is short. Come with something specific.

| Week | Bring |
|:---|:---|
| **4** — Tue Sep 15 | One worked before/after prompt example from the DAIR technique pages, and a question about your project idea |
| **5** — Tue Sep 22 | Three candidate techniques from the Prompt Report taxonomy, and an argument for one |
| **5** — Thu Sep 24 | Your one-page project proposal and ADR-002. **This is the meeting where your project gets defined** — the most important one of the term |
| **10** — Tue Oct 27 | Whatever broke in your RAG build. Debugging together is what the time is for |
| **10** — Thu Oct 29 | A running prototype, however rough, and ADR-004 |

!!! tip "Bring it broken"

    A prototype that does not work is a better use of fifteen minutes than a
    demo that does. If you have spent two days stuck, that is exactly the thing
    to walk in with — I would much rather spend the slot unsticking you than
    watching something succeed.

## Goal setting

The Week 5 and Week 10 meetings end the same way: with a **written goal** for
the next milestone, agreed between us. You leave knowing what "done" means for
the next stretch of work, and the following meeting starts by checking it.

This is the same practice your decision log formalises — say what you intend and
why, then find out later whether you were right.

---

## What still happens that week

**The reading and the work are posted as usual.** The session page carries them
under "Work through this week" instead of "Read before class." Complete it on
your own schedule.

**Discussion moves to the board.** Where a session would have been a
conversation, there is a posted prompt — respond to it, and to at least one
other person.

**Deadlines are flexible.** If something in an individual-meeting week lands
badly, tell me. These are the deadlines I am most willing to move.

The one thing that does not work is silence. A student who emails saying "I am
stuck on the prototype" gets help. A student who says nothing until Week 12 has
a much harder problem.
