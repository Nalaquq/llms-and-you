# Using BoodleBox to understand a reading

The college pays for this. Use it.

Most weeks you will hit a paragraph that does not go in. The useful move is not
to read it four more times — it is to ask something that can explain it back to
you differently, and then to check what it said. This page is how, and where the
line is.

!!! warning "Read this before you use it on coursework"

    **Reading responses and Burchell reflections are no-AI work in this course.**
    Asking a model to explain a passage you did not follow is fine — that is
    reading. Asking it to write your response is not, and it fails the
    assignment. The full policy is in the
    [syllabus](../syllabus.md#using-ai).

    Every other course sets its own rules. Check each one before you use this or
    any other AI tool for it.

## What BoodleBox is

Hampden-Sydney has a contract with BoodleBox. In their own description, it is

> a collaborative AI platform that brings leading AI models together in one
> secure, privacy-first ecosystem. Users can experiment, develop tailored
> solutions, and gain hands-on experience with multiple AI models. The platform
> empowers users to learn, create, and amplify their impact alongside AI —
> helping them build skills and drive impact from classroom to career and
> beyond.

In practice: one login, and behind it models from OpenAI, Anthropic, Google,
Meta, Amazon, Nvidia, and Mistral, plus several hundred specialised bots and the
ability to build your own. Their [FAQ](https://boodlebox.ai/faq) covers the rest.

For this course that matters for one specific reason. When we compare how
different models answer the same question — which we do in Weeks 4, 6, and 8 —
BoodleBox is where you can do it side by side without paying for four
subscriptions.

## 2. Ask it about the thing you did not follow

Open the reading next to it. Be specific about where you stopped — a model given
a vague question gives a vague answer, and you will conclude the tool is useless
when the prompt was.

Weak:

> explain attention

Better:

> I am reading Vaswani et al. 2017, section 3.2.1. I understand that Q, K, and V
> are matrices derived from the input. I do not understand why the dot product
> of Q and K tells you anything about relevance. Explain that step, assume I
> know linear algebra but not machine learning.

The pattern: say what you are reading, say what you already understand, say
exactly where it stopped making sense, and say what to assume about you. That is
four sentences and it is the difference between a useful answer and a Wikipedia
paragraph.

## 3. Ask it to do the thing you would ask a classmate

These work well on course readings:

- *Give me an analogy for this, then tell me where the analogy breaks.*
- *What would I have to already believe for this claim to be true?*
- *This paper is from 2017. What in it has since turned out to be wrong?*
- *Ask me three questions to find out whether I actually understood this.*

The last one is the most useful and the least used.

## 4. Check what it told you

This is not an optional step and it is the actual skill.

Take one specific claim from the answer and find it in the reading. If it is not
there, that is worth knowing — you have just caught a hallucination in the wild,
on material you know something about, which is the best possible time to meet
one. Bring it to class.

Models are confidently wrong about details, and most confident on exactly the
questions where you cannot yet tell. Week 7 is about why.

## 5. Where the line is in this course

| | |
|:---|:---|
| Explaining a passage you did not follow | **Fine.** That is reading |
| Quizzing you on whether you understood it | **Fine**, and underused |
| Comparing how models answer, for a lab | **Fine** — often the assignment |
| Drafting or writing a reading response | **No.** Fails the assignment |
| Drafting a Burchell reflection | **No.** Fails the assignment |
| Anything on a page with a red no-AI banner | **No.** Honor Court |
| Project work, cited | **Fine** — see [acknowledging use](../syllabus.md#acknowledging-use) |

The reasoning, since a rule with a stated purpose is easier to follow: reading
responses measure whether you did the reading and what you made of it. A model
that has not done the reading can still produce a competent-looking response,
which is exactly why one is not acceptable there.

If a situation is not on this list and you are unsure, ask me before rather than
explain after. I have never once been annoyed by that question.
